\CourseSection{Readability Pilot and Supporting Detail}\label{app:readability-pilot}

We ran the method on the official 13,000-skill GitSkills sample to check feasibility and choose the preprocessing.
Of the 13,000 distinct skills, 237 were not named exactly \texttt{SKILL.md}, 1,566 had invalid front matter, 555 were too short (a body under 300 characters or fewer than 30 words of prose), and 2,328 failed a simple English check.
This left 8,314 skills from 5,567 repositories.
Figure~\ref{fig:readability-pilot}a shows why Markdown must become sentences before scoring: on raw text, the median grade rises from 8.6 to 12.2.
Figure~\ref{fig:readability-pilot}b shows that skill sentences are short (a median of 9 words), but Coleman--Liau, which weights word length, places the same text near grade 13, which reflects technical vocabulary.
Descriptions read about four grades harder than bodies. The prose scoring used textstat~\cite{textstat}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,keepaspectratio]{figures/readability-pilot.pdf}
\caption{Readability pilot on 8,314 English skills. (a)~Flesch--Kincaid grade of the body prose, with the Markdown converted to sentences and as raw text. (b)~Body grade under both formulas, and Flesch--Kincaid grade of the description. Boxes show the interquartile range, and whiskers extend to 1.5 times that range.}
\label{fig:readability-pilot}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:readability-pilot}b also shows the two formulas we kept. For code, 5,628 of the 8,314 skills contain a fenced block, 4,304 have at least one complete 10-line window of code, and 886 have a window of bundled script. Tokens came from an approximate language-neutral tokenizer, so scores are indicative. The median linear score was $-12.56$ for code blocks (interquartile range $-18.62$ to $-7.97$) and $-12.19$ for bundled scripts ($-15.32$ to $-9.70$), with a negligible overall difference (Cliff's $\delta = -0.016$). Within the 558 skills having both, scripts scored higher in 62\% of cases, with a median difference of $2.21$, so the paired comparison will be reported as well. Applying the model's sigmoid would place 90\% of code-block skills and 98\% of script skills below 0.01, which motivates the linear output (DD-3). Because the score grows with line count, windows of other lengths are not comparable.
